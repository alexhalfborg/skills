# Meadowlark — customer story voice profile

**This whole file is an invented example, for shape only.** Meadowlark is a brand that does not
exist, and every customer, quote and excerpt below was written for this template. Copy the
structure, never the content.

The team's third-person voice, used for customer transformation and testimonial posts. Read this
together with `shared.md`, which holds the rules that are true of everything the brand publishes.
Nothing here repeats a shared field.

This voice deliberately differs from `personal-post.md` and the two are never loaded together.

Tiers are explained in `shared.md`: ***Ask*** is answered in conversation, ***Infer*** is proposed
and labelled as such, ***Corpus*** comes only from real published writing and is never invented.

---

## Team byline and framing
*Ask*

- **Byline:** The Meadowlark Team.
- **How the brand refers to itself:** "we" and "our" throughout. The post never says "I".
- **How the founder appears:** third person and by role, as "our founder Priya", always with a link
  to her own writing rather than a paraphrase of her opinion. Never put words in her mouth.

## Voice in one sentence
*Infer*

Inferred from the brand's description; nobody has confirmed it.

Someone on the team telling a customer's story carefully, letting the customer do most of the
talking, and being honest about how long it took.

## Point of view
*Infer*

Third person about the customer, who is the central character. "We" and "our" for the brand's own
actions and recommendations. The writer never appears as an individual.

## Customer naming convention
*Ask*

First name from the first paragraph and consistently after that, with the customer's permission
recorded before publication. Honorific and surname where the customer is older or the situation is
formal. Never "one customer" or "a young mum": the named real person is what makes the post
credible.

## Founder cross-link
*Ask*

Priya's own night-shift posts are the natural place to send a reader who wants the founder's
version of the same problem. Link to the specific post rather than the blog index, and only where it
genuinely bears on the customer's situation.

## Greeting and sign-off
*Ask*

- **Greeting:** none. This byline opens straight into the first line.
- **Sign-off:** "The Meadowlark Team" where a piece is sent rather than published.
- **Warmth:** respectful and a step back. Warm about the customer, never chummy about the brand.

## Opener bank
*Corpus only*

Real first lines, tagged by pattern. This voice has two, and both are legitimate here even though
the founder's voice forbids the first.

1. **Definitional lead, customer as illustration.** "Sleep latency is the time between getting
   into bed and actually falling asleep. For most people it is fifteen minutes or so. For Mei Ling
   it was closer to two hours."
2. **Direct customer setup.** "Mei Ling had tried every tea on the supermarket shelf before she
   found us, and she will tell you herself that she did not expect this one to be different."

Never open in the founder's first person. A sensory "you" question or an "I" belongs to the personal
post voice, not to this one.

## Annotated excerpts
*Corpus only*

Short real passages, each followed by one or two sentences saying **why it was chosen and what it
demonstrates**. Aim for three to six, covering different moves rather than different topics.

The `Source:` line is required. An excerpt with no source is not evidence.

#### Handing the story to the customer

Source: "Mei Ling's Two Hours", https://example.invalid/stories/mei-ling

> "I stopped believing any of it would work," she says. "I bought the No. 2 because it was the
> cheapest thing left I had not tried, and honestly I expected to write it off."

**Why this one:** the customer's own scepticism is quoted rather than smoothed away, and it lands
before any result does. A story that starts with belief has nothing to show.

#### Being specific about the timeline

Source: "Mei Ling's Two Hours", https://example.invalid/stories/mei-ling

> Nothing changed for the first fortnight. By the end of the fourth week she was falling asleep
> around forty minutes sooner, and it has held there since, with a bad night perhaps once a week.

**Why this one:** a real timeline including the part where nothing happened, plus a named remaining
limit. This is the honesty rule doing visible work, and it is what stops the post reading as an
advertisement.

#### The brand in the third person

Source: "What Changed For Daniel", https://example.invalid/stories/daniel

> We suggested he move the tea an hour earlier rather than change the blend. It is the first thing
> we ask about now, because it turned out to matter more than which one he was drinking.

**Why this one:** "we" is the company acting, never a single writer with an opinion, and the brand
admits it learned something from the customer rather than the other way round.
