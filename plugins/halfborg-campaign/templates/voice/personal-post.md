# Meadowlark — personal post voice profile

**This whole file is an invented example, for shape only.** Meadowlark is a brand that does not
exist, Priya Raman is not a real person, and every excerpt below was written for this template.
Copy the structure, never the content.

The founder's first-person voice. Read this together with `shared.md`, which holds the rules that
are true of everything the brand publishes. Nothing here repeats a shared field.

Tiers are explained in `shared.md`: ***Ask*** is answered in conversation, ***Infer*** is proposed
and labelled as such, ***Corpus*** comes only from real published writing and is never invented.

## Founder persona and bio
*Ask*

Priya Raman started Meadowlark in 2021 after fifteen years as a night-shift nurse. She blends and
sells two evening teas, writes most of what goes on the blog herself, and is open about the fact
that she still sleeps badly perhaps one night in ten.

The bio matters because it is where the authority comes from. Name the thing that gives this person
the right to be listened to on this subject, and keep it to a sentence or two.

## Voice in one sentence
*Ask*

A tired, practical person telling you what actually worked for her, with the caveats left in.

This is the target every paragraph is measured against. Keep it short enough to hold in mind while
drafting.

## Voice era boundary
*Ask*

Posts from March 2023 onward reflect the current voice. Anything earlier was written by an agency
and reads nothing like her, so it is not a style reference even though it carries her byline.

If the byline has never drifted, write "none" here.

## Point of view
*Infer*

Inferred from the brand's description; nobody has confirmed it.

"I" for anything she lived through or tried. "We" only when she means herself and the reader
together, working through the same problem; the company is never "we" under this byline. "You"
generously, and always paired with something she has actually seen rather than a lecture.

## Greeting and sign-off
*Ask*

How a piece under this byline opens and closes when it is addressed to someone, such as an email or
a letter-style post.

- **Greeting:** "Hi <first name>," (informal, first name only, no title)
- **Sign-off:** "Sleep well, Priya"
- **Warmth:** close. She writes like someone who has met you once and remembers it.

Use the real thing if the brand has one. If nobody has said, write "not set" rather than inventing
a signature under someone's name.

## Opener bank
*Corpus only*

Real first lines, each tagged with the pattern it is an example of. The patterns are the writing
skill's; the lines are the brand's.

1. **Sensory question.** "Have you ever lain there doing sums about how much sleep you would get
   if you fell asleep right now?"
2. **News or research hook.** "A sleep study out of Melbourne last month said something I have
   been telling customers for three years, and I want to be careful about how I say it."
3. **Her own memory.** "For most of 2019 I finished a shift at seven in the morning and lay awake
   until noon."
4. **Customer pattern.** "Three people this week have asked me the same question about magnesium,
   so it is probably worth answering properly."
5. **Recognition or shared calculation.** "Two dollars a night for something that might work. You
   have probably done that arithmetic on something recently."

## Sentence-level tells
*Corpus only*

Characteristic phrasings, quoted as written. These read as authentic rather than clichéd. Use them
where they fit and never force one in.

- "I will be honest with you."
- "That took me longer than it should have."
- "Your mileage will vary, and I mean that literally."

## Literary devices this founder uses
*Corpus*

Which moves this particular writer actually reaches for, with a real example of each. The generic
catalogue lives in the writing skill; this is the subset that is hers.

- **Domestic analogy.** Explains a mechanism through something in the reader's kitchen.
  "Caffeine does not leave your body when you stop noticing it, the way a kettle stays hot after
  you switch it off."
- **Personification of the body or a system.** "Your nervous system is still waiting for a text
  message."
- **Rhetorical question mid-post**, not only in the opener, used to hand the reader a decision.

## Annotated excerpts
*Corpus only*

Short real passages, each followed by one or two sentences saying **why it was chosen and what it
demonstrates**. This is the most useful thing in the file: a writing skill can copy a technique from
a passage it can see, and cannot copy one from an adjective.

Aim for three to six. Cover different **moves** rather than different topics: one opener, one piece
of technical explanation, one metaphor, one moment of honesty or caution.

The `Source:` line is required. An excerpt with no source is not evidence, and it is what lets the
voice era boundary above be applied to an excerpt.

#### Opening on the reader's own situation

Source: "The Two O'Clock Sums", https://example.invalid/blog/two-oclock-sums

> Have you ever lain there doing sums about how much sleep you would get if you fell asleep right
> now? Five hours and forty minutes. Then five and a half. I did that arithmetic most nights for
> about two years, and it never once helped me fall asleep faster.

**Why this one:** the opener puts the reader inside a specific behaviour before naming any problem,
then earns the right to advise by admitting she did it too. Note that no product appears for
several paragraphs.

#### Explaining a mechanism without jargon

Source: "What Is Actually In The No. 1", https://example.invalid/blog/whats-in-no-1

> L-theanine is an amino acid, which is a building block your body already knows what to do with.
> It does not switch you off. It takes the edge off the alertness, the way turning down a radio is
> different from unplugging it.

**Why this one:** the term is introduced, defined in six plain words, then explained through a
household comparison. This is the reading-level rule from `shared.md` working in a real sentence.

#### Naming a limit before it is asked for

Source: "What Is Actually In The No. 1", https://example.invalid/blog/whats-in-no-1

> I should say the obvious thing. If you are lying awake because something in your life is genuinely
> wrong, tea will not fix that, and I would rather you spent the eighteen dollars on something else.

**Why this one:** the honesty move that carries the whole voice. She names the limit unprompted and
in the same register as the rest of the piece, without hedging and without softening it into a
disclaimer.

#### The regional register, at its dose

Source: "Three Questions About Magnesium", https://example.invalid/blog/magnesium-questions

> The powder ones, the taste is the problem. Everything else about them is fine.

**Why this one:** one topic-comment construction, once, in an aside. The rest of the post is
standard English. This is the whole dose for a piece of that length.

## Worked customer-story callout
*Corpus only*

The shape a short customer blockquote takes inside a practical guide. One real example is worth more
than a rule.

> Mei Ling had been buying supermarket chamomile for years and getting nothing from it. She switched
> to the No. 2 in January and told us she was falling asleep about forty minutes sooner by the end of
> the month. [Her full story is here.](https://example.invalid/stories/mei-ling)

First name only, one specific outcome, a real timeline, and a link to the full story. If there is no
real customer to feature, leave a placeholder rather than inventing one.
